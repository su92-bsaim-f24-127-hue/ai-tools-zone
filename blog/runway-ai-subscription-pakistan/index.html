Gen-3 is retired: Runway's current model is Gen-4.5

One change matters more than any price. Runway retired Gen-3 Alpha on 8 July 2026 and Gen-3 Alpha Turbo on 30 July 2026 — the models a generation of guides (and older copies of this topic across the web) describe, with their Motion Brush and Gen-3 camera tools, are no longer available on Runway at all. Our own catalog listing still carries the 'Runway Gen-3' name from when Gen-3 was current, which is exactly why we tell buyers to confirm on WhatsApp which models their access includes before ordering.

Runway's current flagship is Gen-4.5, launched in December 2025: text-to-video and image-to-video in clips of 2 to 10 seconds, at 12 credits per second of generated video. Below it, Gen-4 and Gen-4 Turbo remain available for cheaper draft work, and a paid Runway subscription also opens third-party models inside the same workspace, including Veo 3.1, Kling 4.0 and Seedance 2.5. Paid plans remove watermarks and include 4K upscaling; Pro adds a custom voice clone, and Max adds early access to new models.

What the credits actually buy

Runway prices generation in credits, and the honest unit to compare is seconds of finished video on the current model. On Gen-4.5 at 12 credits per second, Runway's own pricing page works out Standard's 625 credits as about 52 seconds of video a month, Pro's 2,250 credits as about 187 seconds, and Max's 9,500 credits as about 791 seconds. The same credits stretch roughly twice as far on Gen-4 Turbo (104, 375 and 1,900 seconds respectively), which is how most working editors use their plan: drafts on Turbo, finals on Gen-4.5.

Two credit rules catch people out. On Standard and Pro, unused monthly credits do not roll over — they reset each billing cycle; only Max rolls unused credits forward, and only by one month. And if you run dry mid-project, top-up credits are sold in blocks of at least 1,000 and, once bought, never expire. Parallel generation — how many videos and images you can render at once — is 5 on Standard, 15 on Pro and 20 on Max.

Paying Runway directly from Pakistan: the real cost

Subscribing direct is possible with a Pakistani bank card that has international and recurring transactions enabled — but the dollar price is not what leaves your account. On top of the interbank conversion, your bank adds its exchange margin and foreign-transaction charges, plus FBR Section 236Y advance tax: 0.5% if you are on the Active Taxpayers List, 1% if you are not, for Tax Year 2027. Ignore guides quoting 10% or 16% for Section 236Y; those figures are invented. The usual reasons a card still declines are mundane: international or recurring use is not switched on, the bank declines recurring foreign debits, the 3D Secure step does not complete on the foreign checkout, or the balance covers the converted price but not the margin, fees and tax on top. Our guide to paying for ChatGPT Plus from Pakistan walks through the same fixes, including the State Bank's USD 30,000 yearly card cap, and they apply to Runway in the same way.

That maths is the honest comparison: Standard direct costs at least ≈ PKR 4,148 a month before margin, fees and tax (or ≈ PKR 3,318 at the annual rate, paid a year upfront), against PKR 3,500 flat locally with delivery and warranty included.
